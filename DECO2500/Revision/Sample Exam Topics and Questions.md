**Asks to identify the individual problems and issues, and present improvements that the website could make in order to abide and reduce the gulfs of evaluation & execution.**

*Sarah, a frequent traveller, wants to book a flight for her upcoming business trip. She logs into her preferred airline's website on her laptop. Upon reaching the homepage, she finds the booking section. However, she notices that the layout has changed since her last visit, and the options seem a bit confusing.*

*Sarah wants to book a flight departing from Brisbane and arriving in Melbourne on a specific date. However, the website's interface doesn't provide clear labels for each field, making it difficult for her to locate the departure and arrival city fields. She has to spend extra time deciphering the interface and figuring out how to input her travel details.*

*After finally entering her travel details, Sarah proceeds to search for available flights. However, the search results page displays a long list of flights with minimal filtering options. The information is overwhelming, and Sarah finds it challenging to quickly identify the best option for her trip.*

*The flight options are presented in a dense format, making it hard for Sarah to compare prices, departure times, and airline preferences at a glance. Additionally, the website lacks interactive features like sorting or filtering options based on her preferences, such as direct flights or specific airlines. As a result, Sarah struggles to evaluate the available choices efficiently. She feels frustrated by the website's lack of user-friendly design that makes the booking process more time-consuming than necessary.*

**Write the answer below here.**

**[[Gulf of Execution]]:**

1. **Unclear interface mapping:** The changed layout and missing field labels make it difficult for Sarah to identify how to perform her intended action (enter departure and arrival cities). This widens the gulf of execution because the system’s affordances don’t clearly indicate what actions are possible or how to execute them.
    
2. **Poor action visibility:** The booking section lacks clarity in what to click or fill out next, meaning the steps to achieve her goal (booking a flight) are not visible or intuitive.
    

**[[Gulf of Evaluation]]:**

1. **Overwhelming feedback with minimal interpretability:** The dense, unorganised flight list provides feedback (search results) but makes it hard for Sarah to interpret whether the output aligns with her intent (finding the best flight).
    
2. **Lack of filtering and comparison features:** Without interactive tools to evaluate results (like sort/filter by price, time, or airline), Sarah cannot easily assess if her goals have been met, widening the gulf of evaluation.
